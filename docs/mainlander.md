This file is Claude's own translation of selected passages from volume one of Philipp Mainländer's Philosophy of Redemption (1876). It was made from a scanned German text with reading errors. No published English translation was consulted.

The German source is kept outside the repo. Line numbers refer to lines of that source file. Page numbers are those of the 1876 edition, where the scan shows them.

The wiki quotes are as the pages stood on 27 Nov 2026, after the law of the weakening of force was renamed and reworded that day.

This is a reference for checking design proposals against Mainländer's own words. It is not settled design. Nothing here is a decision.

How to read it:

- Passages are grouped by theme. Each gives the book section and paragraph number, the source lines, and the translation.
- The translations aim to be faithful, not elegant. Technical terms are kept consistent. The German term is given in square brackets on first use only.
- An ellipsis in square brackets marks text left out.
- Each "Bears on" line names the wiki pages concerned. It says whether Mainländer agrees with the wiki, differs from it, or says nothing the wiki uses. It reports and does not judge the design.
- Wiki pages are named by their page title. They were read on 27 November 2026.

There are 72 passages in 12 themes.

## 1. Everything is individual will, and its essence is motion

### 1.1 The foundation stone
Physics §1, p. 49. Line 511.

> I take as the foundation stone of physics not the species that hovers invisibly between heaven and earth, the metaphysical concept of kind without marrow or sap; still less the so-called physical forces such as gravity, electricity and so on; but the real individual will to live [individueller Wille zum Leben] won in the Analytic. We grasped it in the innermost core of our being as that which underlies force [Kraft] (which is knowable from outside), and since everything in nature acts without cease, and activity [Wirksamkeit] is force, we are entitled to conclude that every thing in itself is individual will to live.

**Bears on:** Will in motion, Force. Agrees: every thing is an individual will, and force is that will seen from outside.

### 1.2 Motion is the will's only genuine predicate
Physics §2, pp. 49 to 50. Lines 516 to 518.

> Further, life and motion [Bewegung] are interchangeable concepts; for where there is life there is motion and the reverse, and a life that was not motion could not be grasped by human thought.
> Motion is also the explanation of life; for motion is the known or felt mark of life.
> Motion is therefore essential to the will to live; it is its only genuine predicate, and we must hold to it in order to take the first step in physics.

**Bears on:** Will in motion. Agrees with "Everything in the Dreamlands is a will in motion".

### 1.3 One undivided motion, or a resultant of split motions
Physics §2, p. 50. Lines 521, 522 and 525.

> If the individual will has one unitary, undivided motion, because it is itself whole and undivided, then as object it is an inorganic individual. Of course only the drive [Trieb], the inner motion within a particular individuality, is meant here.
> If on the other hand the will has a resultant motion, which arises from its having split itself, then as object it is an organism. The part that has been separated off is called an organ. […]
> Sensibility (and so mind [Geist] too) is therefore nothing more than a part of the motion essential to the will […]. There is only one principle in the world: individual will to live, and it has no other beside it.

**Bears on:** Will in motion, Coherence. Agrees that a will has its own particular motion, and that a complex will's motion is a resultant of part-motions. Mainländer's split is into organs within one living will; he does not here describe a will "breaking apart into separate, simpler motions" (for that see 7.2 and 7.4).

### 1.4 Feeling is not part of the will's essence
Physics §4, pp. 51 to 52. Lines 538 and 543.

> To the essence of the will, accordingly, belongs only motion, and not representation, feeling [Gefühl] and self-consciousness, which are appearances of a particular split motion. […]
> The will to live is therefore to be defined as an originally blind, vehement urge [Drang] or drive, which through the splitting of its motion becomes knowing, feeling and self-conscious.

**Bears on:** Will in motion, Mood, Held and manifest. Differs. The wiki says "Feelings are how that motion is felt from inside. So every will has a mood, its current feeling" and "you can love and hate at once, and so can a door". For Mainländer only wills whose motion has split into sensibility (animals and humans) feel; plants and inorganic things have states of motion but no feeling (see 9.2 and 9.3).

### 1.5 From inside I always feel only one state
Physics §5, p. 53. Line 554.

> In myself I always find only the individual will to live in one particular motion, one particular state [Zustand], of which I am conscious. I receive only the resultant of many activities; for inwardly I am not in a knowing relation. […] I always feel only one state of my will.

**Bears on:** Mood, Held and manifest, Will in motion. Agrees that feeling is the will's motion felt from inside. Differs on simultaneity: the wiki says a mood is "a volume of every emotion at once" and "Opposites can pinch at once, so they coexist and never net out". Mainländer says one state is felt at a time, the resultant.

### 1.6 The body is the will made object
Physics §5, pp. 53 to 54. Lines 558 and 562.

> The human body is object, that is, it is the Idea [Idee] man that has passed through the forms of knowledge. Independently of the subject, man is pure Idea, individual will. […]
> The whole organism, by contrast, is the objectification of the whole will: it is the unfolding of the whole will. From this point of view the whole organism is the will's sphere of force [Kraftsphäre], become representation, objectified […].

**Bears on:** Will in motion. Agrees with "The body, like everything in the Dreamlands, is a will in motion too".

### 1.7 A stone wills as a man does
Physics §34, pp. 97 to 98. Lines 933 and 936.

> We have seen that there is only one principle in the world: individual, self-moving will to live. Whether I have before me a piece of gold or a plant, an animal, a human being, is all the same with regard to their essence in general. Each of them is individual will, each lives, strives, wills. What separates them from one another is their character, that is, the way in which they will life, or their motion. […]
> The stone, like the human being, wants to exist, wants to live. Whether life is there a simple dark drive, here the result of many activities of a unitary will that has come apart into organs, is, with regard to life alone, all the same.

**Bears on:** Will in motion. Agrees with "creatures, fire, water, doors, even stone". Note that he equates a thing's character with its motion.

### 1.8 Without inner motion a thing would be nothing
Physics §19, p. 72. Line 744.

> Nothing can be more mistaken than to deny life to a chemical Idea. In the same moment in which a piece of iron, for example, lost its inner motion, which is after all the only mark of life, it would not, say, fall apart, but would in fact become nothing.

**Bears on:** Will in motion, Stillness, Calm. Agrees that a will never truly stops moving (the wiki's Stillness keeps its force "still moving"). For Mainländer real rest would be annihilation.

## 2. Force: what it is

### 2.1 Force is the sum of a thing's activities
Analytic (paragraph number not checked; the section on matter). Line 177.

> Over against it [matter, as a form of the understanding] there stands, completely independent, the sum of the activities of a thing in itself or, in a word, force.

**Bears on:** Force. Partly agrees. The wiki says "Force (Kraft, Mainländer's term) is the amount of a will's motion: the area of its mood's circle". Mainländer's force is the whole of a thing's activity, its essence seen from outside; he does speak of its intensity, but the "area of a circle" measure is not his.

### 2.2 The force unveiled in our core is the will to live
Analytic, closing paragraphs, pp. 44 to 45. Lines 487, 490 and 492.

> What, now, is the force that unveils itself in the core of our inner being? It is the will to live. […]
> This will is a developing individuality, which is identical with the self-moving sphere of activity found from outside. But it is through and through free of matter. […]
> As the most important result of the Analytic we hold firmly in hand the individual, self-moving will to live, totally independent of the subject.

**Bears on:** Force, Will in motion. Agrees: force and will are one thing, known from outside and from inside.

### 2.3 Every force came into being
Analytic §26, p. 30. Lines 374 and 376.

> […] that all forces have come into being; for what they were in the transcendent domain, in the simple unity [einfache Einheit], wholly escapes our knowledge. […] Further we can say apodictically that in the simple unity they were not force; for force is the essence, the essentia, of a thing in itself in the immanent domain. […]
> Although everything that is did not arise from nothing but already existed before the world, yet everything that is, every force, did arise as force, that is, it had a definite beginning.

**Bears on:** Force, Weakening of force. The wiki says force is "never created". Mainländer agrees that nothing adds force once the world exists (see 4.1), but holds that all force had a beginning with the world.

### 2.4 The universe is a finite whole of finite spheres of force
Analytic (paragraph after §27, number not checked), pp. 34 to 35. Lines 404 and 410.

> […] recognised that these forces stand in the most intimate dynamic connection, and so arrived at a totality of forces, a firmly closed collective unity [Collectiv-Einheit]. […]
> The universe is not one single force, a simple unity, but a whole of finite spheres of force. […] A totality of finite spheres of force, however, must necessarily be finite.

**Bears on:** Force, Cosmology. Agrees with "Each will has its own force, and the world's force is the sum of theirs".

### 2.5 The universe as one sphere of force in which each acts on all
Physics §30, p. 85. Line 857.

> If we look at the universe, immeasurably great but finite, a single sphere of force shows itself to us, that is, we gain the concept of a collective unity of countless individual Ideas, each of which acts on all the others and at the same time undergoes the activity of all the others. This is the dynamic connection [dynamischer Zusammenhang] of the universe […].

**Bears on:** Cosmology, Force. Agrees with a world force that is the sum of individual forces. The universal mutual action has no named counterpart on the pages read.

## 3. The simple unity before the world, and its death

### 3.1 The unity can only be described negatively
Analytic §25 (inferred: the passage directly precedes the heading 26), p. 29. Lines 366 and 367.

> We can therefore determine the simple unity only negatively, namely, from our present standpoint, as: inactive, unextended, undifferentiated, unsplintered (simple), motionless, timeless (eternal).
> But let us not forget, and hold quite fast, that this enigmatic, utterly unknowable simple unity has perished together with its transcendent domain and no longer exists.

**Bears on:** The Dreamer, Cosmology. Agrees that the origin was one and is "unknowable". Differs on whether it still exists: the wiki's Dreamer is a present being who dreams, watches and answers.

### 3.2 Unity, rest, freedom against multiplicity, motion, necessity
Physics §37, pp. 106 to 107. Lines 989 and 992 to 996.

> […] we always find it [any object in nature] in an unceasing striving, in an incessant inner motion. But motion was foreign to the transcendent unity. […] We must therefore ascribe absolute rest to the pre-worldly unity. […]
> The immanent schema:
> world of multiplicity [Vielheit], motion, necessity
> is thus faced by the transcendent schema:
> simple unity, rest, freedom.

**Bears on:** Cosmology, The Dreamer. Differs. The wiki says "the Dreamer dreamt one unified dream in the beginning". For Mainländer the unity did not move, act or dream before the one act of disintegration; all motion belongs to the world of the many.

### 3.3 The unity was neither will nor mind
Physics §37, pp. 107 to 108. Lines 1000 and 1001.

> So we are forced to declare that the simple unity was neither will, nor mind, nor some peculiar interpenetration of will and mind. […] But now we also have the right to give this being the familiar name that has always designated what no power of imagination, no flight of the boldest fantasy, no abstract thinking however deep, no collected, devout heart, no enraptured spirit carried away from the earth has ever reached: God.

**Bears on:** Cosmology, Shard, The Dreamer. Differs. The wiki says "Every being and place is a shard of that will" and a shard is "a fragment of the Dreamer's original will". Mainländer denies that the original unity was a will at all; will exists only in the world of the many.

### 3.4 God has died, and his death was the life of the world
Physics §38, p. 108. Lines 1004 to 1006.

> But this simple unity has been; it is no more. Changing its essence, it has splintered itself wholly and completely into a world of multiplicity. God has died, and his death was the life of the world.
> In this lie, for the thoughtful thinker, two truths that deeply satisfy the mind and lift the heart. First, we have a pure immanent domain, in or behind or above which there dwells no force, call it what one will, that makes the individuals do now this, now that, as the hidden director of a puppet theatre does the puppets. Then we are lifted by the truth that everything that is existed in God before the world. […]
> Further, we are no longer in God; for the simple unity is destroyed and dead. Instead we are in a world of multiplicity, whose individuals are bound into a firm collective unity.

**Bears on:** The Dreamer, Attention, Cosmology. Differs, and this is the largest difference. The wiki says "The Dreamer is a vast, cosmic and unknowable being whose dream is the Dreamlands ... the Dreamer is the only god-figure", who "grows" Reveries and whose Attention "adds up flares". Mainländer's God no longer exists, and nothing stands in, behind or above the world. Agrees that the one became the many ("It fractured").

### 3.5 The first motion was the disintegration, and every later motion continues it
Physics §32, p. 94. Lines 912 and 913.

> The first motion and the origin of the world are one and the same. The transformation of the simple unity into the world of multiplicity, the passage of the transcendent into the immanent domain, was precisely the first motion. […] from our present standpoint we must characterise it simply as the disintegration [Zerfall] of the simple unity into a world of multiplicity.
> All following motions were only continuations of this first one, that is, they could be nothing other than again disintegration, or further splintering of the Ideas.

**Bears on:** Cosmology, The Dreamer. Agrees with "It fractured, and is still fracturing".

### 3.6 God's one free act: the world as the means to non-being
Metaphysics §4 and §5, pp. 322 to 325. Lines 2699, 2708 and 2719.

> We may, however, make them [will and mind] regulative principles for "the mere judging" of the act, that is, we may try to explain the origin of the world to ourselves by regarding it as if it had been a motivated act of will. […]
> Only one single act was therefore possible for God, and a free act […], namely to enter into absolute nothingness, into the nihil negativum, that is, to annihilate himself completely, to cease to exist. […]
> The one act of God, the disintegration into multiplicity, accordingly presents itself as the carrying out of the logical act, of the resolve not to be, or in other words: the world is the means to the end of non-being, and indeed the world is the only possible means to that end. God recognised that only through the becoming of a real world of multiplicity, only by way of the immanent domain, the world, could he pass from super-being [Uebersein] into non-being.

**Bears on:** Cosmology, The Dreamer. The wiki gives no reason for the fracturing, so this says nothing the wiki uses. Mainländer stresses that speaking of God's will here is only an "as if".

### 3.7 The seven results
Metaphysics §7, pp. 326 to 327. Lines 2733 to 2740.

> 1) God willed non-being;
> 2) his essence was the obstacle to immediate entry into non-being;
> 3) the essence had to disintegrate into a world of multiplicity, whose individual beings all have the striving for non-being;
> 4) in this striving they hinder one another, they fight with one another and in this way weaken their force;
> 5) the whole essence of God passed over into the world in altered form, as a definite sum of force [Kraftsumme];
> 6) the whole world, the universe, has one goal, non-being, and reaches it through continuous weakening [Schwächung] of its sum of force;
> 7) every individual is brought, through weakening of its force, in the course of its development to the point where its striving for annihilation can be fulfilled.

**Bears on:** Force, Weakening of force, Cosmology. Agrees with a fixed starting sum and "a world moving towards its end". Differs on how force is lost: the wiki says force is "lost only when opposed manifest motions annihilate"; Mainländer's point 4 says individuals weaken their force by hindering and fighting one another, with no condition that they be opposites.

## 4. The weakening of force (no conservation of the sum)

### 4.1 Force lost has no replacement
Physics §32, p. 95. Lines 917 and 918.

> But is the essence of any individuality still the same as it was at the beginning of the world? No! It has changed. Its force has lost in intensity: it has become weaker.
> This is the great truth that geology teaches. A gas is, in its innermost essence, in its drive, stronger than a liquid, and this stronger than a solid body. Let us not forget that the world has a finite sphere of force, and that therefore any Idea whose intensity slackens cannot be strengthened again without another Idea losing force. A strengthening is indeed possible, but always at the expense of another force, or in other words, when in the struggle of the inorganic Ideas one of them is weakened, the sum of force objectified in the universe is weakened, and for this loss there is no replacement, precisely because the world is finite and entered existence with a definite force.

**Bears on:** Force, Weakening of force, Mood. Agrees that force is never added and that a will gains only at another's expense (wiki: "every living thing exists at the expense of others"). Agrees that the sum only falls: the wiki says "The world's total force never grows". Differs on how: the wiki says force is "lost only when opposed manifest motions annihilate"; in Mainländer any weakening in any struggle is a net, unreplaced loss.

### 4.2 A body that stimulates another loses force by doing so
Physics §30, pp. 85 to 86. Lines 858 and 859.

> […] motion is the only predicate of the individual will, and the most varied states of a particular will are merely modifications of its normal motion. There is neither free heat nor free electricity, and no bound (latent) heat either. If a body is warm and loses its heat to another, this means only that it has heightened the state of the other and, in exercising the stimulus [Reiz], has lost force, that is, has weakened its own state.

**Bears on:** Force, Mood (and the Stimulus page they cite). Partly differs. The wiki says "A stimulus changes the shape of a mood without moving any force". Mainländer agrees that nothing travels from one thing into the other (the receiver changes its own state, see 6.2), but says the thing giving the stimulus loses force in giving it.

### 4.3 The loss is too slow to see
Physics §32, p. 96. Line 921.

> It is no different even now in the inorganic realm. The Ideas fight one another unceasingly. New combinations arise without interruption, and these are again forcibly separated, but the separated forces at once unite with others, partly compelling, partly compelled. And here too the result is weakening of force, although, because of the slow development, it does not lie open to view and escapes perception.

**Bears on:** Force, Cosmology. Agrees with "The world's force diminishes over aeons, too slowly for most inhabitants to notice". Differs on the cause, as in 4.1: combining and separating in general weaken force.

### 4.4 Each generation is weaker
Physics §33, pp. 96 to 97. Lines 925 and 928.

> […] for the stronger does usually always win, but in each new generation the stronger individuals are less strong, the weaker ones weaker, than in the one before. […]
> So in the organic realm, as in the inorganic, we see one basic motion: disintegration into multiplicity, and here as there, as first consequence, strife, struggle, war, and as second consequence, the weakening of force.

**Bears on:** Cosmology. Agrees with the idea the page lists as not yet a decision, "force weakening every generation". The wiki's sentence "wills join as well as split" is not contradicted (see 7.1), but Mainländer calls splitting the basic motion.

### 4.5 The organic realm is a more perfect form for weakening force
Physics §34, p. 100. Lines 952 and 953.

> Disintegration into multiplicity, life, motion: all these expressions designate one and the same thing. […]
> The organic realm is only a higher stage of the inorganic; it is a more perfect form for the struggle for existence, that is, for the weakening of force.

**Bears on:** Force, Cosmology. Says nothing the wiki uses directly. It differs from the wiki's two named channels of loss ("Along seams ... opposed ambient emotion annihilates; with Forgetting"): for Mainländer living itself is the main channel.

### 4.6 The Physics's result: indestructible world, continuously weakening sum
Physics §38, pp. 109 to 110. Lines 1013, 1014 and 1018.

> 1) The simple chemical forces are indestructible;
> 2) Real motion had a beginning, but it is endless. […]
> The world is indestructible, but the sum of force contained in it weakens continuously, in the course of an endless motion.

**Bears on:** Weakening of force, Force. Here Mainländer speaks as if the sum were kept. This is his provisional statement; 4.7 revises it.

### 4.7 The Metaphysics's correction: the series end in nothing
Metaphysics §19, pp. 341 to 342. Line 2869.

> […] and so have to declare that the result of the Physics was an essentially one-sided one. The whole universe moves, continuously weakening its force, out of being into non-being, and the series of development, to which we already had to give a beginning in the Analytic, will also have an end: they are not endless, but issue into pure absolute nothingness, into the nihil negativum.

**Bears on:** Cosmology, Inspirations, Weakening of force. Agrees with "a world moving towards its end" and "the resultant motion of the whole world runs towards the Dream's end". Agrees that the sum only falls. Differs on how it is lost, as above.

### 4.8 The law of the weakening of force
Metaphysics §21, p. 346. Line 2902.

> We have seen that one single great law has governed nature from the beginning, governs it and will govern it until its annihilation: the law of the weakening of force. Nature is growing old.

**Bears on:** Weakening of force. Agrees on the name and the direction: the wiki's law is "Weakening of force", named after his, and says "the world's total force never grows". Differs on the cause: the wiki says force "is lost only when opposed manifest feeling annihilates into lucidity"; his law has it lost in every struggle. The German phrase for "conservation of force" does not occur in volume one.

### 4.9 Organisms draw things in and put them out weakened
Metaphysics §16, p. 338. Line 2834.

> […] that the whole realm of organisms is only a better form for the deadening [Abtödtung] of the sum of force active in the universe. Every organism follows its drive, but in doing so it is a serving member of the whole. It is a form that […] lets chemical Ideas in, draws them into the vortex of its individual motion and then expels them, no longer as the same, but weakened, even though the weakening escapes observation and reveals itself to connecting perception only at the end of great periods of development.

**Bears on:** Force, Mood. Agrees in part with "A will regains force mainly by eating: every living thing exists at the expense of others": an organism sustains itself on what it takes in. He adds that what passes through is weakened, so eating is itself a loss to the world's sum; the wiki does not say this.

## 5. The will to death, fate and the end of the world

### 5.1 In the inorganic realm the will is will to death
Metaphysics §10, pp. 329 to 330. Lines 2762, 2764 and 2767.

> And now I ask: how must the will of the chemical Idea now be mirrored in the human mind? As will to live? In no way! According to all that has gone before, it is pure will to death [Wille zum Tode]. […]
> Life is not willed, but is only the appearance of the will to death […]. Life in the inorganic realm is always only appearance, is the gradual motion of the chemical Ideas towards death. […]
> The chemical Idea wills death, but can attain it only through struggle, and therefore it lives: in its innermost core it is will to death.

**Bears on:** Will in motion, Cosmology. Says nothing the wiki uses: none of the pages read gives wills a will to death.

### 5.2 Everything is will to death, veiled as will to live
Metaphysics §14, pp. 334 to 335. Lines 2802 and 2808.

> To sum up: everything in the world is will to death, which in the organic realm appears, more or less veiled, as will to live. Life is willed by the pure plant drive, by instinct and finally demonically and consciously, because in this way the goal of the whole, and with it the goal of every individuality, is reached more quickly. […]
> But at bottom the immanent philosopher sees in the whole universe only the deepest longing for absolute annihilation, and it is to him as if he clearly heard the cry that pierces all the heavenly spheres: Redemption [Erlösung]! Redemption! Death to our life! and the consoling answer to it: you will all find annihilation and be redeemed.

**Bears on:** Cosmology, Inspirations. Agrees that the world moves towards its end. Says nothing the wiki uses about that end being willed by every thing and being redemption.

### 5.3 Fate is the resultant of all individual motions
Physics §38, p. 109. Lines 1009 and 1010.

> The disintegration into multiplicity was the first motion, and all motions that followed it, however far they may diverge, entwine, seemingly tangle and untangle again, are only its continuations. The one motion of the world that again and again, continuously, results from the actions of all individuals standing in dynamic connection is the fate [Schicksal] of the universe. […]
> […] so fate is nothing other than the becoming of the world, the motion of the Orphic conjuncture, the resultant of all individual motions.

**Bears on:** Cosmology. Agrees closely with "the resultant motion of the whole world runs towards the Dream's end".

### 5.4 The last state: nothing but liquids, then nothing
Metaphysics §19, pp. 344 to 345. Lines 2884, 2885, 2887 and 2888.

> All gases have vanished from the universe, that is, the tenacious sum of force has weakened to such a degree that only solid and liquid bodies still make up the universe. […]
> Absolutely nothing now stands in the way of the redemption of these liquids. Each has a free path: every conceivable part of them passes through the ideal point and its striving is fulfilled, that is, it is annihilated in its innermost essence. […]
> Then God has in fact passed from super-being, through becoming, into non-being […].
> First the transcendent domain perished; now (in our thoughts) the immanent one has passed away too; and we look, according to our view of the world, appalled or deeply satisfied, into absolute nothingness, absolute emptiness, into the nihil negativum.

**Bears on:** Cosmology. Agrees that the world has an end. The wiki speaks of "the Dream's end, far beyond any player's reach" and does not say what the end is.

## 6. Individuality: closed, sovereign, and only half independent

### 6.1 Individuality is closed being-for-itself
Physics §8, p. 57. Line 580.

> If we turn to the will of man, we have first to determine his individuality as a whole. It is closed being-for-itself [geschlossenes Fürsichsein] or egoism (selfishness, I-ness). Where the I stops, the not-I begins […].

**Bears on:** Coherence. Says nothing the wiki uses in these words. It is the nearest Mainländer comes to what holds an individual together: individuality is a closed sphere, and he gives it no measure that rises and falls like Coherence. The same phrase is used of animals (line 707), plants (line 731) and chemical Ideas (line 742).

### 6.2 A motive only stimulates; the will changes its own state from its own force
Physics §9, pp. 59 to 60. Lines 618 and 619.

> They [the states] are all to be traced back to the transformation of the normal state which the will brings about under the prompting of a corresponding motive [Motiv]. Nothing mysterious, supersensible, foreign penetrates its individuality, maintains itself and rules in it […]; for individuality is sovereign in its own house. As the chemical force is impenetrable, so man is a closed sphere of force, which can indeed be compelled from outside to show itself now in this way, now in that, to pass now into this state, now into that; but the motive always effects only a prompting, and the will reacts solely according to its nature, its character, from its own force.

**Bears on:** Force, Mood, Held and manifest, Quality of the will. Agrees with "A stimulus or a motive changes the shape but not the area" and with "Qualities decide which reaction a motive gives rise to". Differs from spells carrying force into a target: the wiki says "A Raw spell that lands carries force: it grows the target's circle" and "what you send, they carry". For Mainländer nothing foreign enters an individuality; it only ever changes its own state.

### 6.3 The dead unity survives as the bond between individuals
Physics §36, p. 105. Lines 980 and 981.

> Thus there lies over the world of multiplicity, like a reflection as it were, the pre-worldly unity; thus one invisible, unbreakable bond as it were embraces all individual beings, and this reflection, this bond, is the dynamic connection of the world. Every will acts on all others directly or indirectly, and all other wills act on it directly and indirectly […].
> So we have the individual that is half independent, half active from its own force, half suffering through the other Ideas. It intervenes sovereignly in the development of the world, and the development of the world intervenes in its individuality.

**Bears on:** The Dreamer, Cosmology, Shard. Agrees that all things come from one origin and remain connected. Differs in that the connection is only a "reflection" of a unity that is gone, where the wiki has a present Dreamer.

### 6.4 Individual fate, chance, and the choice made before the world
Metaphysics §24 and §25, pp. 352 to 356. Lines 2962, 2981 and 2985.

> Here it is individual fate (the individual course of life) and shows itself as the product of two factors of equal weight: the particular individual (demon [Dämon] and mind) and chance (the sum of the activity of all individuals). […]
> Everything that is was in the simple pre-worldly unity. Everything that is has accordingly, speaking figuratively, taken part in God's resolve not to be, has in him made the resolve to pass over into non-being. […]
> The world is the free act of a pre-worldly unity; but in it only necessity rules, because otherwise the goal could never be reached.

**Bears on:** The Dreamer, Cosmology. Says nothing the wiki uses.

## 7. Forces combining, holding together and coming apart

### 7.1 Chemical combination: conquest, and a third thing in which both live on bound
Physics §23 and §32, pp. 77 and 94. Lines 781 and 914.

> If the excitation is a matter of a widening of the individuality, the force becomes positively electric, otherwise negatively electric […]. The process resembles an act of rape far more than a loving union. The one individuality wants a new motion, another life in a third thing; the other resists with all its might, but is conquered. In any case the chemical combination is the product of a begetting. In what is begotten both individuals live on, but bound, so that it shows quite different properties. […]
> Every simple chemical force had the craving to widen its individuality, that is, to change its motion, but met the same craving in every other, and so arose the most fearful struggles of the Ideas against one another in the most vehement, most excited state. The result was always a chemical combination, that is, the victory of the stronger over a weaker force and the entry of the new Idea into the unceasing struggle.

**Bears on:** Held and manifest, Lucidity, Emotion, Cosmology. Agrees with "wills join as well as split". Differs on what happens when opposed states meet. The wiki says "Wherever opposites meet out in the shared world, they annihilate into lucidity". In Mainländer the oppositely excited forces (positive and negative electricity) do not annihilate: one conquers the other and both live on, bound, in a new individual. Nothing like lucidity is produced.

### 7.2 A combination's life is holding its motion; its death is the return to simpler motions
Physics §25, pp. 79 to 80. Lines 805, 808 and 809.

> If one simple chemical Idea combines with another, a new Idea with a character of its own arises. […] and yet the single Ideas are wholly contained in the combination. […]
> The life of the chemical force consists in persisting in a particular motion or, when circumstances are favourable, in the expression of the desire for a new motion […]. The persisting is made possible only by constant defence, and already here the truth clearly emerges that life is a struggle.
> The death of the chemical combination shows itself, finally, as a return of the simple substances that were bound in it to their original motion.

**Bears on:** Coherence, Will in motion. Agrees closely with "Coherence is ... how well a will keeps its own particular motion instead of breaking apart into separate, simpler motions". Mainländer states it as a two-way fact (it persists or it dies), with no graded value.

### 7.3 A compound would feel itself as one thing
Physics §34, pp. 98 to 99. Lines 938, 940 and 943.

> They are totally bound, and in their combination something wholly new. If ammonia (NH3) had self-consciousness, it would feel itself neither as nitrogen nor as hydrogen, but as unitary ammonia in one particular state. […]
> Seen from here there is no difference at all between a chemical combination and an organism. Both are a unity in which a certain number of simple chemical Ideas are fused. […]
> […] the type [Typus] of a combination rests on the bound individuals, it stands and falls with them, it does not hover above them.

**Bears on:** Coherence, Mood, Held and manifest. Agrees that a composite is one will with one motion of its own. Bears also on collective wills ("including collective wills such as cities"): Mainländer's examples of fused unities are compounds and organisms; he calls the world and the earth collective unities, and in volume one I found no passage that treats a city or state as one individual will. Differs again on simultaneity: the compound feels "one particular state", not its parts' states side by side.

### 7.4 When an organism dies, the bound forces go free without loss, but the higher force is dead
Analytic §27, pp. 32 to 33. Lines 386, 389, 392 and 393.

> If on the other hand we take a chemical combination, for example hydrogen sulphide, this force is already perishable. It is neither sulphur nor hydrogen, but a third thing, a sphere of force firmly closed in itself, but a destructible force. If I break it down into the basic forces, it is annihilated. […]
> If an organism dies, the forces bound in it become free again without the slightest loss, but the force that until then ruled the chemical forces is dead. Is it supposed to live on, separated from them? […] Nature, the only source of truth, alone can give information, and nature says: they are dead. […]
> 1) all simple chemical forces are, so far as our experience reaches up to now, indestructible;
> 2) all chemical combinations and all organic forces, by contrast, are destructible.

**Bears on:** Force, Weakening of force, Coherence. Partly agrees with "When a form that holds force dissolves, the force is not lost". The bound forces are freed "without the slightest loss". Differs in two ways: the higher force that was the form is simply dead, and the freed forces return to their own motions (7.2); they do not keep the dead form's feeling, where the wiki says the force "keeps its emotion but loses the form that held it" and "hangs where it happened as ambient emotion". It also differs from "At zero Coherence you return as an Echo": for Mainländer the individual that has dissolved does not return.

### 7.5 The organism keeps its type while its matter passes through; at death the type falls apart
Physics §28 and §34, Metaphysics §17; pp. 83, 99 and 339. Lines 843, 944 and 2841.

> Every organism dies, that is, the Idea is destroyed. The type, which during life, persisting through change, assimilated to itself the simple chemical Ideas constituting it and separated them out again, itself falls apart. […]
> An organism, by contrast, separates out of the combination now this substance, now that, and assimilates the replacement to itself, while constantly maintaining the type […]. […]
> Now the elements of which the type is composed persist at its death. They lose the typical stamp, the typical particularity, intervene anew in the general cosmic life, form chemical combinations or enter other organisms, whose life they sustain.

**Bears on:** Coherence, Force, Mood. Agrees with a form that holds its own motion and falls apart into simpler things, and with eating as what sustains life. The line that elements "lose the typical stamp" differs from freed force that "keeps its emotion", as in 7.4.

## 8. The states of the will: the normal state and the eight motions

### 8.1 States are felt motions, and are distinct from qualities
Physics §9, p. 58. Line 613.

> The states of the will are to be strictly distinguished from the qualities of the will [Willensqualitäten]. In them alone, as I have often said, do we grasp our innermost being. We grasp it immediately and do not know it. Only when we bring our states, which are nothing other than felt motions, into reflection do we become knowing, and the states at the same time objective for us. Thus we […] infer, by directing the greatest attention to those motives which at all times set our will into a particular motion, from the constantly recurring states to the constitution of our character, whose traits I have named qualities of the will.

**Bears on:** Emotion, Will in motion, Quality of the will. Agrees with "An emotion is one of eight motions of the will" and "Feelings are how that motion is felt from inside", and with qualities as the standing character behind passing emotions.

### 8.2 The normal state
Physics §9, p. 59. Lines 615 and 617.

> The basic state from which we must start is the normal feeling of life [das normale Lebensgefühl]. We do not, as it were, feel ourselves at all; the will is completely content: nothing disturbs its clear mirror, neither pleasure nor displeasure. […]
> All other states of the will rest on this normal one (which one might also call equanimity [Gleichmuth]) and are only modifications of it.

**Bears on:** Calm, Mood, Stillness. Agrees with Calm as the resting state that the emotions modify. Mainländer's own names are the normal state and equanimity; "Calm" is the wiki's word. Note that he already says of the normal state "we do not feel ourselves at all", which is close to what the wiki reserves for Stillness ("its motion is no longer felt from inside").

### 8.3 The eight principal modifications, in four pairs
Physics §9, p. 59. Line 618.

> The principal modifications are: joy and grief [Freude und Trauer], courage and fear [Muth und Furcht], hope and despair [Hoffnung und Verzweiflung], love and hate [Liebe und Haß] (affects). The last are the strongest; they are modifications of the highest degree.

**Bears on:** Emotion. Agrees exactly with the eight emotions "in four opposed pairs" and with their pairing. Mainländer adds that love and hate are the strongest; the wiki's table calls Love "a violent surge" and Hate "the hardest rush", which fits, but does not rank the pairs. He says these are the principal ones among many (see 8.6).

### 8.4 The will as a sphere; the normal motion circles the centre
Physics §10, p. 60. Lines 623 and 624.

> In the normal state the will moves, as it were, like a calmly flowing stream. If we picture the will under the image of a sphere [Kugel], the motion would be an even, ring-shaped one around the centre: a circling that is at rest in itself.
> All the other motions mentioned, by contrast, stream either from the centre to the periphery, or the reverse. The difference lies in the manner in which the path is covered.

**Bears on:** Calm, Emotion, Mood, Force. Agrees with "Calm is the will circling at rest" and with emotions as outward or inward motions. Differs in the picture: the wiki's mood is "a circle whose area is the will's total force" which "Each emotion pinches ... in its own direction on the wheel". Mainländer's image is a sphere with flow between centre and periphery; he gives no directions round a wheel, no lobes, and does not equate the sphere's size with an amount of force here.

### 8.5 The eight motions described
Physics §10, pp. 60 to 61. Lines 625, 627 to 634.

> Joy is a leaping, jerking welling-forth from the centre, now strong, now weak, in waves now broad, now short. […] To the joyful person his individuality is too narrow […].
> Courage is a calm, composed streaming-out in short, regular waves. The courageous person steps firmly and surely.
> Hope, by contrast, always covers the path in one wave. It is a blissful, light motion out from the centre. […]
> Love I compare to a vehement surging-up from the centre to the periphery; it is the most powerful streaming-out: the waves tumble over one another and form eddies. The will would like to break through its sphere, it would like to become the whole world.
> Hate, on the other hand, is the most intense streaming-back of the will from the periphery to the centre, as if every extension were repugnant to it and it could not have the dear I concentrated, pressed together and compressed enough. Like an army in flight, feeling balls itself together.
> Despair covers the path to the centre as if with one leap. The person, abandoned by all, convinced that there is no rescue for him any more, flees into his innermost core, to the last thing he can clutch, and this last thing breaks too. […]
> Fear is a trembling motion inwards. The individual would like to make itself as small as possible, it would like to vanish. […]
> In grief the will moves in large, regular waves towards the centre. One seeks oneself out, one seeks in one's innermost the comfort one can find nowhere.

**Bears on:** Emotion (and the Emotions sheet). Agrees almost line for line with the wiki's table: Joy "bursts out", Courage "steady short waves out", Hope "one light lift out", Love "a violent surge out", Hate "the hardest rush in", Despair "one sudden drop in", Fear "trembling in". One small difference: the wiki's Grief is "large, slow waves in" (sheet: "Large, slow, regular waves inward"); Mainländer says large and regular, and does not say slow. The colours, adverbs, wings of the house and intensity names are not in Mainländer.

### 8.6 "Mood" as a word for state; many gradations
Physics §10 and §12, pp. 61 to 63. Lines 635 and 653.

> For state one often puts mood [Stimmung] and says: he is in a solemn, hopeful, courageous, sad mood; one also says out of tune [mißgestimmt], to indicate that the circling motion no longer runs regularly. […]
> Between the principal states listed above there are many gradations; besides these there are many other states, which I however pass over […].

**Bears on:** Mood. Partly agrees: "mood" is his everyday word for the will's current state. Differs in sense: for him a mood is the one state the will is in, where the wiki's mood is "a volume of every emotion at once".

### 8.7 Pleasure is expansion, displeasure concentration
Physics §14, pp. 64 to 65. Lines 680, 683 and 685.

> Immanent philosophy unites all states of the will in the concepts pleasure and displeasure [Lust und Unlust]. Pleasure and displeasure are immediate states of the demon; they are whole, undivided motions of the genuine will to live or, expressed objectively, states of the blood, of the heart. […]
> The states of pleasure are expansion, those of displeasure concentration of the will. I already indicated above that in the former states the individual would like to go out of itself and show the whole world how blissful it is. […]
> In the states of displeasure, by contrast, the individual is thrown back upon itself.

**Bears on:** Emotion, Grain. Agrees with "Pleasant emotions move outward, unpleasant ones inward". Partly agrees with "Outward feelings spread wide to neighbours": pleasure wants to show and share itself with the whole world. The second half, "inward ones strike one target deep", is not in Mainländer, who says displeasure throws the individual back on itself. Note also "whole, undivided motions", which sits with 1.5 against several emotions at once.

### 8.8 Love widens the sphere, hate narrows it, and certain qualities give rise to each
Physics §11, pp. 61 to 62. Lines 639, 640, 643, 644, 646 and 648.

> Quite generally one can say that in love man strives to widen his individuality, in hate on the contrary essentially to restrict it. But since neither the one nor the other can be brought about, the individual can only seek to enlarge or to limit its outer sphere of activity.
> Man widens his individuality first demonically through the sexual drive (sensuality [Wollüstigkeit]) […].
> He further widens his outer sphere and puts himself into the state of love through the quality of the will imperiousness [Herrschsucht] or ambition. […]
> Then love shows itself as love of money, on the basis of avarice [Geiz]. […]
> Friendship too is to be mentioned here, which rests on the quality of the will fidelity [Treue]. […]
> The individual on the contrary narrows its outer sphere and puts itself into the state of hate through envy [Neid].

**Bears on:** Quality of the will (and the Qualities sheet). Agrees with "Qualities decide which emotions come most naturally to you", and with four of the sheet's feeds: Sensuality, Imperiousness and Fidelity feed Love, Envy feeds Hate. Mainländer gives quality-to-state links only for love and hate (he also names love of fame, line 645). The sheet's other feeds, and Calm and Stillness qualities, have no source in this passage.

### 8.9 Double motions: anger
Physics §12, p. 63; Aesthetics §29 (inferred from position), p. 157. Lines 655, 656 and 1410.

> We must, however, consider a second kind of motions of the will, which I will call double motions [Doppelbewegungen], in distinction from the simple motions examined so far.
> In hate the individual withdraws to its innermost core. […] Now if the hate is very great, it often flips over into the opposite motion, that is, the will suddenly streams to the periphery, not however to embrace lovingly, but to annihilate. This motion is anger, rage, the furor brevis. […]
> Every state is to be traced back to a particular inner motion, either to a simple one or to a double motion.

**Bears on:** Emotion, Outburst, Held and manifest. Says nothing the wiki uses: the pages read have no double motions. It is the nearest thing in Mainländer to opposed motions in one will, and he makes them successive (inward, then flipping outward), not simultaneous. It is also the nearest thing to an outburst, but it is hate turning outward, not excess feeling escaping. Other double motions he names: aesthetic enthusiasm (line 1072), the sublime state (line 1168), humour (line 1203), the comic state (line 1240) and moral enthusiasm (line 1716).

## 9. States below the human: animals, plants, inorganic things

### 9.1 Animals have fewer and weaker states
Physics §16, pp. 68 to 70. Lines 714, 715 and 720.

> The states of pleasure and displeasure in the animal, too, are weaker and less numerous than those of man; for their deepening and duration depend on abstract thinking. Only animals of the highest level know the state of joy and of grief. […]
> Further, despair drops out, and only in a few animals will a state of expectation take the place of hope, which presupposes the concept of the future. Every animal, by contrast, knows fear […]. […]
> Like man, the animal too transforms the normal motion into all the other states from its own force.

**Bears on:** Emotion, Quality of the will, Mood. Differs. The wiki says qualities "never limit which emotions it can hold" and treats all eight emotions as open to every will. Mainländer limits which states a will can have by how far its motion has split: no despair or hope in most animals.

### 9.2 Plants: three states, and a character that does not divide into qualities
Physics §18, pp. 71 to 72. Lines 731, 732, 733 and 737.

> The plant is individual will to live and is a closed being-for-itself. It wills life in a quite particular way, that is, it has a character. But this character is very simple. It does not come apart into qualities of the will, but is for all plants, grasped from within, a blind urge, growth of a particular intensity. […]
> One can distinguish only three states in the plant, which correspond to the normal state, to the hate and to the love of the animal, namely growing, blossoming and withering. By withering I here mean concentration. […]
> […] yet this comparison presupposes consciousness, which we must quite decidedly deny to the plant. […]
> The states of the plant's will rest, like all states of the individual in general, on the transformation of its normal motion from its own force.

**Bears on:** Quality of the will, Emotion, Will in motion. Confirms the reading the wiki marks as "not yet checked": the named qualities belong to the human will (and in part to animals, see 10.4); a plant's character "does not come apart into qualities". So "Giving them to things is the game's choice" is accurate, and the wiki's table entry "Growth: Defiance, Sensuality" has no source here. Agrees that a plant has states analogous to the normal state, love and hate; differs from the wiki in denying it consciousness or feeling, and in allowing three states, not eight.

### 9.3 A chemical Idea's character is one simple blind urge
Physics §19 and §21, pp. 72 and 74. Lines 743 and 760.

> The chemical Idea wills life in a particular way, that is, it has a character. Grasped from within, this is an incessant, simple, blind urge. All activities of the chemical Idea are to be traced back to this one urge. […]
> Solid bodies then show gravity, that is, they have one chief striving: to reach the centre of the earth. […] Its special character is the intensity with which it asserts its striving, its cohesion, or also its specific gravity (specific weight).

**Bears on:** Quality of the will, Grain. Differs from "Every kind of thing has qualities, drawn as a fitting subset from the one list below" (for example "Solid: Inflexibility, Obduracy"). For Mainländer an inorganic thing has a character, but it is one urge of a certain intensity, shown in its physical properties, not a set of the human qualities. He does himself compare human characters to solids, waves and vapour as figures of speech (line 773).

### 9.4 Inorganic things have a normal state and modifications of it; heat and electricity
Physics §21 and §23, pp. 74 to 77. Lines 763 to 765, 777, 780 and 781.

> Expansibility through heat says merely that a body, through foreign prompting, has passed into a more excited state, into a more vehement inner motion, and in it seeks to widen its sphere. It has not thereby become warmer because a part of a special Idea called heat has in the most wondrous way penetrated its individuality […], but has become warmer because it has changed its motion, on foreign prompting certainly, but from its very own force […].
> On the other side a body contracts and becomes colder because either the foreign prompting has ceased, or it loses its more excited motion in acting on other bodies. It goes back from the more agitated state into the normal one […]. […]
> The state of aggregation is the normal state of an inorganic body. Every chemical Idea can, on an outer occasion, modify this normal state without losing it entirely. […]
> The chemical Idea in the normal state is indifferent, that is, it shows neither positive nor negative electricity. But if it is stimulated in a certain way, it transforms its state into the positively or negatively electric one.
> If the excitation is a matter of a widening of the individuality, the force becomes positively electric, otherwise negatively electric […].

**Bears on:** Grain, Mood, Emotion, Force. Agrees with a resting state that a thing returns to when the prompting stops (wiki: "against it, the target's mood drifts back towards its grain"), and with outward and inward excitation in things. Differs from grain as "A thing's resting emotion": Mainländer's normal state is by definition not one of the modifications; it is the indifferent state. The line that a body "loses its more excited motion in acting on other bodies" is the same point as 4.2.

### 9.5 All states are modifications of one normal state; the four parallel series
Physics §24, pp. 78 to 79. Lines 794, 795 and 797 to 799.

> […] in the animal kingdom and in man [individuals differ] through greater or lesser unfolding of the will (qualities of the will) and of the mind […].
> All individuals are in incessant motion, and every motion calls forth a particular state. All states are modifications of one normal state, which the will brings about from its own force, and only on foreign prompting.
> The members of the series:
> sexual love, rut, blossoming, positive electricity;
> human hate, animal hate, withering, negative electricity
> are not identical, but they are very closely related to one another.

**Bears on:** Will in motion, Emotion, Held and manifest. Agrees in spirit with one rule for people and things ("so can a door"): things have close relatives of love and hate. Differs in that he says these are "not identical", only related, and he gives things only this one pair.

### 9.6 Light and warmth are appearances of motion
Physics §9 and §30, pp. 59 and 86. Lines 616 and 860.

> One could also call this [normal] state, in the mirror of the subject, the normally warm and mildly shining one; for matter (substance) objectifies the body's impression on our sense of touch as warmth, and the impression of the eyes, in which the inner motion reveals itself so eloquently, it objectifies as bright, mild light. […]
> Light is nothing other than the very vehement motion of the Ideas become visible, or the impression of a vehement motion on the sense of sight, objectified by the subject.

**Bears on:** Emotion (Colours), Calm, Lucidity. Partly agrees: light is a will's motion made visible, and the normal state is "mildly shining" (compare "Calm is soft white"). The wiki's colour per emotion, complementary opposites and "brilliant white flash" of lucidity are not in Mainländer. He also says the eyes only modify ordinary light and are not light sources of their own (line 688).

## 10. Character: egoism, temperament and the qualities of the will

### 10.1 Egoism is the skin of the will; temperament is the most general form of character
Physics §8, p. 57; Ethics §2, p. 169. Lines 583, 589 and 1505.

> The human will, like everything in the world, at bottom first wills existence as such. But then it also wills it in a particular way, that is, it has a character. The most general form of character, which [character] is as it were the inner side of egoism (the skin of the will), is temperament. As is well known, four temperaments are distinguished:
> 1) the melancholic,
> 2) the sanguine,
> 3) the choleric,
> 4) the phlegmatic,
> which are fixed points between which many varieties lie.
> Within the temperament, now, are found the qualities of the will. […]
> This striving appears in man as egoism, which is the husk of his character, that is, of the way in which he wills to live and to maintain himself in existence.

**Bears on:** Temperament, Will in motion, Quality of the will. Agrees on the four temperaments and that the qualities lie within temperament (wiki: "It sits above the qualities"). Differs on the phrase: the wiki says "Temperament is Mainländer's 'skin of the will'". In the German the skin of the will is egoism; character is its inner side, and temperament is the most general form of character. The Ethics repeats this with "husk" [Hülle]. The Mood Meter quadrants and the leanings of each temperament are not in Mainländer (the wiki already says they are the sheet's).

### 10.2 The list of qualities
Physics §8, pp. 57 to 58. Lines 589 to 609.

> The principal ones are:
> envy / good will
> greed / generosity
> cruelty / mercifulness
> avarice / prodigality
> falseness / fidelity
> haughtiness / humility
> defiance / despondency
> imperiousness / mildness
> immodesty / modesty
> baseness / nobility of mind
> rigidity / suppleness
> cowardice / boldness
> injustice / justice
> obduracy / openness
> perfidy / uprightness
> impudence / bashfulness
> sensuality / temperance
> vileness / love of honour
> vanity / holiness
> and between each of these pairs there lie gradations.

**Bears on:** Quality of the will (and the Qualities sheet). Agrees on opposed pairs; the wiki's examples "Envy and Good will, Cruelty and Mercy, Cowardice and Audacity, Fidelity and Falseness" are all his pairs. Differs in number: the wiki says "The sheet lists fifteen pairs"; Mainländer lists nineteen and calls them "the principal ones". All fifteen sheet pairs correspond to pairs of his. The four of his that the sheet lacks are avarice and prodigality, immodesty and modesty, perfidy and uprightness, vileness and love of honour. On names: his word for the wiki's Mercy [Barmherzigkeit] is fairly put as "mercifulness", as the wiki reports; his word paired with imperiousness means mildness [Milde], the wiki's Meekness; his word for the wiki's Pride is nearer haughtiness [Hoffahrt]. On the page reference: the wiki cites "p. 54"; in the 1876 German edition the list is on pp. 57 to 58, so p. 54 is presumably a page of another edition or of a translation.

### 10.3 Everyone has the seed of every quality; qualities are grooves that widen into channels
Physics §8, p. 58. Line 610.

> The qualities of the will are to be regarded as shapings of the will to live in general. They have all sprung from egoism, and since every human being is will to live, which egoism as it were encloses, there lies in every human being the seed of every quality of the will. The qualities of the will are to be compared to scratches, which can widen into channels into which the will flows at the slightest occasion. Yet it must be remarked already here that the human will enters life already as a character. To stay with our image, the infant already shows, beside mere scratches, great hollows; but the former can be broadened and deepened, the latter narrowed and flattened.

**Bears on:** Quality of the will, Temperament, Grain. Agrees closely with "everyone carries the seed of every quality" and with "Each quality has a depth that deepens with use or fades". The channel image also fits "where your mood rests, and which way it drifts": the will flows into its deepest channels. "Leaving remnants" is not said here.

### 10.4 Animals have fewer qualities
Physics §16, pp. 68 to 69. Lines 709 and 711.

> As regards the temperaments and qualities of the will of the animal, it is clear that these must be less numerous than those of man; for its mind is more imperfect, and only in connection with a developed mind can the will shape itself in manifold ways, that is, unfold. […]
> Of the qualities of the will, all those will drop away which have the human mind as their condition, such as avarice, justice, resoluteness, bashfulness and so on. Of the remaining ones, such as envy, falseness, fidelity, patience, gentleness, malice and so on, the apes, elephants, dogs, foxes, horses show the most. Often one can designate the whole character of an animal with one single quality of the will […].

**Bears on:** Quality of the will. Agrees with "Creature: By species" and with a creature having a subset. Differs from the wiki's rule that every kind of thing has qualities from the list: below the animals he gives none (9.2, 9.3). He also names qualities here that are not in his own list of nineteen (patience, gentleness, malice, resoluteness).

### 10.5 Character is inborn and changes only within narrow limits; temperament not at all
Ethics §6, pp. 174 to 175. Lines 1557 to 1559.

> The character of man is inborn, but not unalterable; its alterability, however, moves within very narrow limits, since temperament cannot undergo any change at all and single qualities of the will only in so far as, through early impressing of teachings and through examples, or through the club-blows of fate, through great misfortune and heavy suffering (all of which depends on knowledge, since it can flow into the will only through the mind), a prominent quality of the will can be pressed back down to a mere seed, another awakened and unfolded.
> If the human will were not a knowing one, it would be simply unalterable, like the nature of the chemical force […]. Indeed, as we shall see later, pieces of knowledge can inflame it in such a way that it melts and must be regarded as a totally different one in so far as its deeds are now quite different.

**Bears on:** Quality of the will, Temperament, Grain. Agrees that qualities deepen or fade and that a fading quality sinks back to a seed. Differs in the cause and the range: the wiki says a quality "deepens with use"; Mainländer says change is narrow and comes only by way of knowledge (teaching, example, suffering). He also says a non-knowing will, such as a chemical force, is simply unalterable, where the wiki lets a thing's grain shift "as those qualities deepen". Temperament being fixed is consistent with its being chosen once at character creation.

## 11. Aesthetic contemplation and the feeling of motionlessness

### 11.1 Deep contemplation: the will seems to itself to have stopped
Aesthetics §4, pp. 117 to 118. Lines 1065 and 1066.

> In deep aesthetic contemplation it is to the will as if its usual motion had suddenly ceased and it had become motionless. It is wholly caught in the illusion that it is completely at rest, that all desire, all urge, all pressure has been taken from it and that it is now only a purely knowing being: it is to it as if it bathed in an element of wonderful clarity; it feels so light, so unspeakably well.
> Only completely still objects can put us into this genuine state of deep contemplation. […] At the same time we become timeless, because the motion of our will has wholly vanished from our consciousness and we are wholly sunk in the still object. We live as it were in eternity: through illusion we have the consciousness of absolute rest and are unnameably blissful. If we are disturbed in the deepest contemplation, we awake in the strangest way; for our consciousness does not begin, as after sleep, but motion merely fills it again: we step back out of eternity into time.

**Bears on:** Stillness, Emotion. Agrees closely with "Stillness is the will feeling motionless, after Mainländer's aesthetic contemplation: its force is intact and still moving, but its motion is no longer felt from inside. It is a state, not a loss." Mainländer twice calls the rest an illusion [Täuschung]. He names the trigger as completely still objects, above all calm nature; the wiki's trigger is "be Calm near a marvel", which is the wiki's own. His note that motion "merely fills it again" on waking fits "when Stillness passes your mood resumes".

### 11.2 Sympathetic vibration: the will swings with another's motion
Aesthetics §3 and §4, pp. 117 to 119. Lines 1059, 1070 and 1071.

> But the objectification of an Idea in tones and words is so perfect that the will of the objectifying listener is seized by the motion and swings along with it […]. […]
> In aesthetic sympathy [aesthetisches Nachfühlen], as I already said above, our will swings with the moved will of the object. […] In aesthetic sympathy our will only vibrates softly along, like the string that lies beside a sounding one.

**Bears on:** Held and manifest (shaped feeling), and the Aura and Ambient emotion pages it cites. Says nothing the wiki names, but it is the nearest source for one will taking on a trace of another's motion by nearness (wiki: "Anything that strikes shaped feeling, or stands in it, takes a weak trace of its emotion into its mood"). In Mainländer the listener's own will vibrates; nothing is transferred.

### 11.3 The saint: contemplation made permanent
Ethics §20, pp. 197 to 198. Line 1725.

> […] for his will is to be compared to a clear surface of water that lies so deep that the strongest storm cannot ruffle it. […] They live unspeakably blissful in the present alone, that is, in the feeling of constant inner immobility, even though this is only an illusion; or in other words: the fleeting state of the deepest aesthetic contemplation has in the saint become permanent, it lasts on and on, because nothing in the world is able to move the innermost core of the individual. […] he feels indescribably well in this apparent rest, this lasting inner immobility, although the outer man must still move, feel and suffer.

**Bears on:** Stillness, Quality of the will, Calm. Agrees with the sheet's link "Holiness feeds Stillness" (the holy person lives in lasting contemplation). The wiki says Stillness "is rare"; Mainländer makes it fleeting for most and permanent for the saint.

### 11.4 Sleep: force renewed, and the will stateless
Physics §13, pp. 63 to 64. Lines 662 and 664.

> Sleep is first of all necessary for the organism. The force that is so greatly consumed in dealings with the outer world must be renewed and disorder in the organs removed. Therefore the senses shut themselves off, and the will, wholly confined to its sphere and restless as ever, sets its house in order […].
> Then sleep is necessary for the demon itself. From time to time it must become stateless [zustandslos] in order not to despair; and it can become stateless only in deep sleep.

**Bears on:** Coherence, Force. Partly agrees with "Rest restores it" (Coherence). Differs in what is restored: Mainländer says sleep renews force; the wiki says "A will regains force mainly by eating" and that rest restores Coherence. A "stateless" will has no counterpart on the pages read (Stillness is the nearest, but there the will is awake).

## 12. Mind, knowledge and redemption

### 12.1 Rider and horse; self-consciousness lets the will look into itself
Physics §2 and §3, p. 51. Lines 530, 534 and 535.

> The steerer on the one hand, and the steered together with the unsplit motion on the other, I represent under the image of a seeing rider and a blind horse, which have grown together. […] The rider only proposes the directions; the horse alone determines the direction of its motion. The indirect influence of the mind on the will, by contrast, is of the greatest significance. […]
> The third relation, in man alone, is this, that the steerer through self-consciousness gives the will the ability to look into its innermost being.
> […] Out of the slave who has only to obey there comes first a warner, then an adviser, finally a friend into whose hands the will trustingly lays its destinies.

**Bears on:** Lucid, Lucidity. Says nothing the wiki uses. It is Mainländer's account of what knowing does to a will: the mind cannot command, only propose, and what it lets the will see is its own essence, not that the world is a dream. The rider proposing and the horse choosing has a loose kinship with the wiki's inner voices, which the pages read attribute to Disco Elysium, not to Mainländer.

### 12.2 The will is transformed by knowledge
Ethics §17, p. 193. Lines 1697 and 1698.

> The transformation of the will through knowledge is a fact that philosophy may not pass by; indeed it is the most important and most significant phenomenon in this world. But it is rare. […]
> Knowledge is the condition, namely the clear knowledge of a sure, great advantage that outweighs all other advantages.

**Bears on:** Lucid, and the Belief pages cited from Attention. Says nothing the wiki states. Loosely parallel to the wiki's rule that a belief held with conviction changes what a will can do; in Mainländer firm knowledge or belief (he includes "rock-solid faith", line 1706) changes what the will does, by necessity, not by bending natural law.

### 12.3 Moral enthusiasm: a double motion ending in peace of heart
Ethics §19, p. 196. Line 1716.

> The moral kindling [Entzündung] of the will is a fact […]. It is a fact like the transformation of the normal state of a chemical Idea into the electric one, like the transformation of the normal state of man into the affect. I will call it moral enthusiasm. It is, like the aesthetic, a double motion […]. The first part, drawn together, is a vehement wavering of the will between pleasure and displeasure, called forth by a knowing of genius […]. Its second part, by contrast, is no vehement streaming-out of the will, but pure peace of heart [Herzensfriede]. This peace of heart is capable of heightening […]. It can, namely, under the continued influence of clear knowledge […], heighten itself into:
> 1) moral courage,
> 2) moral joy,
> 3) moral love.

**Bears on:** Calm, Stillness, Emotion. Says nothing the wiki uses. Of note for the wiki's wheel: here the will wavers between pleasure and displeasure (alternation, not coexistence), and a calm state heightens into courage, joy and love, three of the outward emotions.

### 12.4 The philosophical denial of the will to live
Ethics §28, pp. 218 to 219. Line 1887.

> The kindling of the will by the knowledge that humanity is moving out of being into non-being, and by the other, that non-being is better than being, or also by the latter alone, which, independently of the former, can be attained through a clear look into the world, is the philosophical denial of the individual will to live. The will thus kindled wills, until death, the happy state of peace of heart, without interruption, and in death complete annihilation, the full and entire redemption from itself. […] This particular Idea wills to be annihilated, this particular type, this particular form, wills to be broken for ever.

**Bears on:** Lucid, Coherence, Inspirations. Differs from the wiki's Lucid: "someone who knows the world is a dream". Mainländer's one who sees through the world sees that non-being is better than being, wants peace until death and then annihilation; his knowledge is not that the world is a dream. It also differs from "At zero Coherence you return as an Echo" and "Going under ... for a second chance": the redeemed form is "broken for ever". The wiki's Inspirations row takes from him only "a world moving towards its end", not redemption.

### 12.5 The thinker in whom the purpose of existence rises
Metaphysics §13, p. 334. Lines 2798 and 2799.

> In man, accordingly, the will to death, the drive of his innermost being, is no longer simply covered by the will to live, as in the animal, but vanishes completely into the depths, where it expresses itself only, from time to time, as a deep longing for rest. […]
> […] Before the mind of the thinker there rises, radiant and shining, out of the depth of the heart, the pure purpose of existence, while the means vanishes entirely. Now the refreshing image wholly fills his eyes and kindles his will: mightily the longing for death flares up […]. Such a human being is the only Idea in the world that, by willing absolute death, can also attain it.

**Bears on:** Lucid, Stillness. Says nothing the wiki uses. The "deep longing for rest" is the one place where Mainländer ties the wish for stillness to the world's direction.

### 12.6 Affirmation and denial want the same thing; denial is the faster motion
Metaphysics §21, pp. 346 to 347. Lines 2900, 2904 and 2909.

> And here we must declare that the denial of the will to live does not stand in opposition to its affirmation. […]
> In giving himself solely and only to life, always hungry and greedy for life, he acts in the interest of nature and at the same time in his own; for he weakens the sum of force of the universe and at the same time his type, his individuality […]. […]
> So both will the same thing, and both attain what they will; the difference between the two lies only in the kind of their motion. The denial of the will to live is a faster motion than that of affirmation.

**Bears on:** Force, Weakening of force, Coherence. Differs on how force is lost once more: simply living, with full appetite, weakens both the world's sum of force and the individual's own type. In the wiki, living and acting do not reduce the world's force; only annihilation of opposed manifest motions (and Forgetting) does.

## Where the game departs from Mainländer

Each item gives the passage (section number above, with file line) and the wiki line it differs from. This is a list of differences only; several are things the wiki does not attribute to Mainländer in the first place.

1. **How force is lost.** Passages 3.7 point 4 (line 2737), 4.2 (line 859), 4.3 (line 921), 4.9 (line 2834), 12.6 (line 2904). Wiki, Weakening of force: force "is lost only when opposed manifest feeling annihilates into lucidity". In Mainländer force is lost in every struggle, every combination and separation, every exertion of a stimulus, and in living itself. Opposition of kind is not required, and nothing is produced by the loss.

2. **Opposites do not annihilate.** Passage 7.1 (line 781). Wiki, Emotion: "Wherever opposites meet out in the shared world, they annihilate into lucidity." Mainländer's oppositely excited forces fight; one conquers, and both live on bound in a new individual.

3. **The origin is dead, not dreaming.** Passages 3.1 (line 367), 3.4 (lines 1004 to 1006). Wiki, The Dreamer: "The Dreamer is a vast, cosmic and unknowable being whose dream is the Dreamlands ... the Dreamer is the only god-figure." Mainländer: "God has died", and no force dwells in, behind or above the world.

4. **The origin was not a will, and did not move.** Passages 3.2 (line 989), 3.3 (line 1000). Wiki, Cosmology: "the Dreamer dreamt one unified dream in the beginning ... Every being and place is a shard of that will." Wiki, Shard: "any fragment of the Dreamer's original will". Mainländer: the unity was "neither will, nor mind", and was at absolute rest.

5. **The world is real, not a dream.** Passages 3.4 (line 1005), 2.2 (line 492), 12.4 (line 1887). Wiki, Lucid: "You play a Lucid: someone who knows the world is a dream." Mainländer's individuals are real and independent of any subject; what his knower sees is that non-being is better than being.

6. **Feeling is not essential to will.** Passages 1.4 (line 538), 9.2 (line 733). Wiki, Will in motion: "Feelings are how that motion is felt from inside. So every will has a mood, its current feeling". Wiki, Held and manifest: "you can love and hate at once, and so can a door." For Mainländer plants and inorganic things have states of motion but no feeling or consciousness.

7. **Not every will can have every state.** Passages 9.1 (lines 714 to 715), 9.2 (line 732), 9.5 (lines 797 to 799). Wiki, Quality of the will: qualities "never limit which emotions it can hold". Mainländer: most animals lack hope and despair; plants have three states; inorganic things have a normal state and two electric ones.

8. **One state at a time.** Passages 1.5 (line 554), 7.3 (line 938), 8.7 (line 680), 8.9 (line 656). Wiki, Mood: a mood is "a volume of every emotion at once" and "Opposites can pinch at once, so they coexist and never net out". Mainländer: "I always feel only one state of my will"; opposed motions occur in succession, as double motions.

9. **Nothing foreign enters an individuality.** Passage 6.2 (line 618), 9.4 (line 764). Wiki, Mood: "A Raw spell that lands carries force: it grows the target's circle". Wiki, Force: "what you send, they carry." Mainländer: a motive or stimulus only prompts; the will changes its state "from its own force".

10. **The giver of a stimulus loses force.** Passage 4.2 (line 859). Wiki, Force: "A stimulus changes the shape of a mood without moving any force." Mainländer agrees that nothing moves across, but the stimulating body "has lost force".

11. **The skin of the will is egoism, not temperament.** Passage 10.1 (line 583; also line 1505). Wiki, Temperament: "Temperament is Mainländer's 'skin of the will'". Wiki, Will in motion: "Temperament is Mainländer's 'skin of the will'."

12. **Nineteen pairs of qualities, not fifteen.** Passage 10.2 (lines 590 to 608). Wiki, Quality of the will: "The sheet lists fifteen pairs". Four of Mainländer's pairs are absent; all fifteen of the sheet's are his.

13. **Things below the animals have no qualities of the will.** Passages 9.2 (line 731), 9.3 (line 743), 10.4 (line 711). Wiki, Quality of the will: "Every kind of thing has qualities, drawn as a fitting subset from the one list below." The wiki already says "Giving them to things is the game's choice"; the text confirms that reading.

14. **The picture of the will.** Passage 8.4 (line 623). Wiki, Mood: "Every mood is pictured as a circle whose area is the will's total force. Each emotion pinches it in its own direction on the wheel". Mainländer's image is a sphere, with motion circling the centre or streaming between centre and periphery; no wheel directions, no area as amount.

15. **The resting state is not an emotion.** Passages 8.2 (line 617), 9.4 (line 780). Wiki, Grain: "Grain is a thing's resting emotion". Mainländer's normal state is the indifferent state that all the others modify; characters differ in which channels the will flows into (10.3), not in resting at an affect.

16. **What survives a dissolved form.** Passages 7.4 (line 389), 7.5 (line 2841). Wiki, Weakening of force: "its force is freed as ambient emotion: it keeps its emotion but loses the form that held it." Mainländer: the bound forces go free without loss and return to their own motions, losing "the typical stamp"; the higher force is dead.

17. **No return after dissolution.** Passages 7.4 (line 389), 12.4 (line 1887). Wiki, Coherence: "At zero Coherence you return as an Echo". Mainländer: a dissolved individual lives on only in offspring; otherwise its form is "broken for ever".

18. **Sleep renews force.** Passage 11.4 (line 662). Wiki, Force: "A will regains force mainly by eating". Wiki, Coherence: "Rest restores it". Mainländer has sleep renew the force consumed in dealing with the world.

19. **Qualities change through knowledge, within narrow limits.** Passage 10.5 (line 1557). Wiki, Quality of the will: "Each quality has a depth that deepens with use or fades". Mainländer: change is narrow, comes only through the mind, and a non-knowing will is unalterable.

20. **Grief is not said to be slow.** Passage 8.5 (line 634). Wiki, Emotion: "Grief: large, slow waves in". Mainländer: "large, regular waves". A small difference of wording.

21. **Page reference.** Passage 10.2. Wiki, Quality of the will: "about p. 54 of *The Philosophy of Redemption*" and "p. 54". In the 1876 German edition the list of qualities is on pp. 57 to 58.

## Not found

Ideas on the wiki pages read for which I found no passage in volume one. Most of these the wiki does not attribute to Mainländer; they are listed so that nobody goes looking.

- **Attributed to Mainländer on the wiki, and not found as stated:**
  - Temperament as "skin of the will" (Temperament, Will in motion). The phrase exists (line 583) but names egoism. See departure 12.
  - "Force (Kraft, Mainländer's term) is the amount of a will's motion: the area of its mood's circle" (Force). The word is his; "amount of motion" and "area" as its measure are not. He speaks of a force's intensity and of a sphere of force.
  - "p. 54" for the list of qualities and for "mercifulness" (Quality of the will). Not the page in this edition. "Mercifulness" is an English rendering, so it could not be checked against the German beyond the German word he uses (line 592).
- **Everything attributed to Mainländer in Inspirations was found:** everything is will in motion (1.1, 1.2, 1.7), feelings as motions (8.1, 8.5), qualities of the will (10.2, 10.3), a world moving towards its end (4.7, 5.4). Stillness "after Mainländer's aesthetic contemplation" was found (11.1).
- **Not attributed to Mainländer, and not in the text:**
  - Lucidity as a product of opposites annihilating, and its dissolving of weakly held forms (Lucidity).
  - The distinction between held and manifest feeling (Held and manifest).
  - A watching Dreamer and its Attention (Attention, The Dreamer).
  - Burning a memory, being Ablaze, and an outburst of excess (Burning, Outburst). The nearest things are anger as a double motion (line 656) and the figure of speech "to burst with rage" (line 772).
  - Coherence as a graded value with a maximum, and anything like mood capacity as a cap per emotion (Coherence, Mood capacity).
  - Memories and beliefs as stores of force, material, renown (Force, Coherence).
  - A colour for each emotion and complementary opposites (Emotion).
  - Adverbs, intensities and intensity names for the emotions (Emotion).
  - "Inward ones strike one target deep" (Emotion, Grain). Only the outward half has a source (line 683).
  - Humility and Temperance as qualities that feed the resting state (Calm). Both are in his list (lines 595 and 606), but he links no quality to the normal state.
  - A city or other collective as a single will with its own state (Coherence, Will in motion). He uses "collective unity" for the world and the earth, and speaks once of "great national individualities" in passing (line 1867), but I found no statement in volume one that a collective is one individual will. The Politics part (lines 1939 to 2658) was only searched by keyword, not read through, so this is the least certain "not found".
