# Principles

Rules of thumb the designer set for reasoning about the design. Use them in any design work: theorising, a viva, resolving questions, a bake-off.

They are not game terms and not part of the game's ontology, so they have no wiki page and never appear by name on one. The weakening of force is a hard constraint that nothing can break. These are shorthand for how we reason, and a case may be an exception.

A principle is a reason, not evidence. Give one to explain a suggestion or an objection. Never cite one as a settled rule, and never treat one as proof. Where a principle and a settled page disagree, say so: that is a question for the designer.

Only the designer adds or changes a principle.

## Attention

- **Inception principle:** Attention is drawn from a will when that will believes the rules of its dream are being broken by something. Named after the film, not after the Glossary term inception. The Attention page states the settled rule.
- **Neo principle:** as a rule of thumb, only the Lucid is aware that the world is a dream, and so only the Lucid can act against its rules.
- **What follows:** events that can happen with no Lucid present ought to be following the rules the will believes in. The test: can it happen regularly, and between characters other than the player? If it can, it does not draw Attention by itself.
- **The exception:** some dealings between other characters may be rare or overt enough to draw Attention all the same.

## Mainländer

- **Mainländer principle:** check a proposal about the will, force or emotion against Mainländer's own text. Where it departs from him, say so. Departing is allowed; it should be known, not accidental.
- **The text:** `docs/mainlander.md` holds Claude's English translation of the passages that bear on the game, from volume one of his main work, the Philosophy of Redemption (1876). Start there. The German source is kept outside this repo; the file's line numbers point into it. The scan has reading errors and the translation is Claude's, so a quote is a reason, never proof.
